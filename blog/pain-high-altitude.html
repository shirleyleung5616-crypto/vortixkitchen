Selling Cookers to High-Altitude Markets? Why 'Food Won't Cook' Isn't a Defect

📅 Sep 5, 2026⏱ 2 min read✍️ Vortix Kitchen

Ship cookers to Almaty, Bishkek or the Kyrgyz highlands and buyers call: "food won't cook." You refund — but the unit is fine. It's physics.

Above 1,500 m water boils near 95°C; above 3,000 m near 90°C. Lower boil = longer cooking, undercooked rice and stews, and a warranty claim you didn't earn.

Put this in your PO for altitude markets

  • Higher wattage: spec 3000–3500W+ so water recovers heat fast after food goes in — less time stuck at the low boil.
  • Boost / max-power mode: a high setting for altitude, not just an eco mode.
  • Altitude note on the carton for Central Asia highlands: "boils at lower temp above 1500 m — use higher power." Stops the "defect" call before it starts.
  • Lead with induction: ~90% of energy hits the pot, so more reaches the food than gas at altitude.

Right spec = fewer altitude returns, real reorder in mountain markets.

Selling into mountain markets?

Tell us your altitude and buyer type — we'll spec higher-wattage units with a boost mode and an altitude note on the carton, so the returns don't start.
