Cooker Certifications for the Middle East: What Your Shipment Needs in 2026

📅 Aug 21, 2026⏱ 8 min read✍️ Vortix Kitchen

The Middle East is one of the fastest-growing markets for induction and infrared cookers — but customs is strict, digital, and unforgiving. A container that sails through in one Gulf port can be seized in another for a missing mark. The good news: the region runs on a mostly unified system, so one well-planned certification package opens Saudi Arabia, the UAE and the wider GCC.

This guide maps the real, current certification requirements for cookers heading to the Middle East, and gives you a checklist to use with any factory.

Why certification is non-negotiable for Middle East importers

  • Customs clearance: Gulf ports block uncertified electrical appliances at the border — no mark, no release.
  • Liability: if an uncertified unit causes a fire, the importer — not the factory — is on the hook locally.
  • Market access: major distributors and government tenders require recognized Gulf marks before they will list your product.
  • Brand trust: traceable test reports are what separate a real manufacturer from a trading company with a sticker printer.

The backbone: GCC G-Mark (GSO)

The G-Mark, governed by the Gulf Standardization Organization (GSO), is the foundation of Middle East market access. It is mandatory across the seven GCC states — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain and Yemen — for low-voltage electrical appliances, and induction and infrared cookers are explicitly on the强制 (mandatory) list.

  • One certificate, seven countries: a single G-Mark approval clears customs across the whole Gulf — no need to repeat testing per country.
  • Validity: 3 years from issuance; no factory audit and no annual fee.
  • Marking: the G-Mark (the Arabic letter جيم / "jim" inside a circle) must be printed on both the product and the outer packaging, together with the certificate number.
  • Accepted evidence: a valid CB report (within 3 years) plus Gulf national deviations is the fastest route; testing is against IEC 60335-1 / IEC 60335-2-9 family standards with GSO EMC (CISPR) additions.
  • Documentation: English + Arabic user manual is required, and the rating label must show the Gulf电网 specification: 230V / 50Hz.

Saudi Arabia — SABER / SASO

Saudi Arabia is the largest single market in the region and the strictest. On top of G-Mark, every regulated product must be registered on the SABER platform run by SASO:

  • PC (Product Certificate): confirms the model meets Saudi technical regulations — valid ~1 year.
  • SC (Shipment Certificate): issued per shipment; it is the document customs actually checks at the port.
  • Critical rule: since 2025, goods must already hold SABER certificates before arrival — the old "undertaking letter" clearance path is gone. Plan 3–6 weeks ahead.
  • Energy label: applicable categories also need the Saudi energy-efficiency label (Arabic + English) on the unit and box.

United Arab Emirates — ECAS (ESMA)

The UAE requires ECAS registration through the ESMA / UAE.S system for controlled electrical products. For cookers this typically means:

  • A valid test report (CB or GCC-recognized lab) covering safety + EMC.
  • ECAS certificate registration, with the ECAS / EQM mark applied where required.
  • Arabic + English labeling, including the UAE energy-label format where applicable.

Because the UAE also honors the Gulf G-Mark, a clean G-Mark + ECAS combo covers both the UAE market and the wider GCC.

Other Gulf states (Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain)

These markets lean on the same G-Mark as the base. Some add a national clearance step (for example Kuwait's KUCAS, Oman's conformity registration, Qatar's conformity certificate), but a valid G-Mark dramatically shortens each process. The practical sequence for most importers is: get G-Mark first, then layer on Saudi SABER and UAE ECAS as needed.

Hard requirements that trip up first-time importers

  • Voltage & frequency: the Gulf runs on 230V / 50Hz. Spec your units for this — not 110V.
  • Plug type: the region uses the BS 1363 British three-pin plug. Units with other plugs need adapters or re-work at import.
  • Language: user manual, warnings and rating labels must be English + Arabic. Submissions in English only are rejected.
  • Country of origin: "MADE IN CHINA" must be clearly shown on the export carton.

The "CE sticker" trap

Some suppliers flash a CE mark and call it certified. For the Middle East that is meaningless — CE alone will not clear customs in Jeddah or Dubai. What actually matters is the G-Mark (and, for Saudi/UAE, SABER/ECAS). Always ask for the traceable test report (issuing lab, report number, date) and verify it covers your exact model — not a similar one.

Your 5-point certification checklist when talking to a factory: (1) Does this exact model hold G-Mark — and can you see the certificate number? (2) Show me the test report, not a photo of a sticker. (3) Who issued it — a GSO-recognized lab (Intertek, TÜV, SGS, BV)? (4) Is Saudi SABER / UAE ECAS registration included in the lead time? (5) Who handles destination-market registration — you or the factory?

A simple buyer's certification checklist

  • Name the exact destination country in your PO — "the Middle East" is not a customs code.
  • Request the test report PDF, not a photo of a sticker.
  • Confirm G-Mark is included in the production timeline, then budget 3–6 weeks for Saudi SABER if you sell to KSA.
  • Lock 230V / 50Hz + BS 1363 plug + Arabic/English manuals in the specification before tooling.
  • Keep a warranty clause tied to certified batches.
