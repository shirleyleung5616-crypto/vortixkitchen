The 5 Returns That Eat Your Margin — and the Exact Spec Line That Stops Each

📅 Aug 21, 2026⏱ 6 min read✍️ Vortix Kitchen

A returned cooker isn't just one lost sale — it's the refund, the freight back, the angry review, and the buyer who doesn't reorder. Across 15+ years of OEM/ODM cooker production, the same five failures generate almost all the returns we see in the trade. The good news: each one maps to a line you can write into your purchase order, so a serious factory is contractually blocked from shipping it to you. Here they are.

1. Dead on arrival — PCB / IGBT failure

The IGBT module and control board are the heart of the unit. Underspecced or poorly soldered boards die within months, especially in hot markets — and a dead unit is a 100% refund.

Spec line to add: "IGBT: branded module (Infineon / ST or equivalent); 100% power-on burn-in test ≥ 30 min per unit; AOI-inspected soldering. Batch burn-in record provided."

2. Cracked glass on arrival

Low-grade ceramic glass or weak shipping cushioning means cracks before the box is even opened — pure loss, no second chance.

Spec line to add: "Glass: tempered microcrystalline, Schott/Eurokera grade or verified equivalent; sea-freight carton validated by drop test; corner protectors mandatory."

3. "Slow heating" complaints — coil inefficiency

Aluminum-clad or poorly wound coils lose efficiency and overheat, and customers describe the symptom as "it doesn't heat properly." That's a return even when the unit "works."

Spec line to add: "Heating coil: 100% copper; winding spec verified per batch; coil-to-glass gap tolerance checked."

4. Shuts down mid-cooking — cooling failure

In hot-climate markets (across Africa and Southeast Asia), a weak fan or missing thermal cutoff means the unit throttles and then cuts out during service. This is the #1 complaint in those regions.

Spec line to add: "Cooling: high-CFM fan with NTC thermal control and auto-shutoff; validated in chamber test at 40°C ambient, continuous duty."

5. Blocked at customs — fake or missing certification

A "CE" sticker with no real test report won't clear customs in your markets, and exposes you to liability. This isn't a return — it's a stranded container.

Spec line to add: "Certification: valid national mark for [exact destination country]; traceable test report (lab, number, date) covering the exact model supplied."

Your copy-paste PO quality clause

Drop this block into your order and a compliant factory will know you're not a first-timer:

Supplier shall supply units with branded IGBT + 100% burn-in, tempered microcrystalline glass with drop-tested sea carton, 100% copper coil, high-CFM thermal-controlled cooling validated at 40°C continuous duty, and valid [country] certification with traceable test report. Batch test records to be provided with shipment.
