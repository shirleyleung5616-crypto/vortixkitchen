Winter 'Dead Screens' in Russia & Central Asia — a Cold-Chain Gap, Not a Defect

📅 Sep 12, 2026⏱ 2 min read✍️ Vortix Kitchen

Winter sea-and-land shipments into Russia and Central Asia can sit at −30°C for days. Units arrive with black or scrambled LCDs and brittle plastic — your buyer calls it a defect, files a return, and your brand takes the hit. It isn't a quality failure; the components fell below their rated storage temperature.

Three specs to put in your PO

  • "Rated storage temperature: −25°C (or −30°C) for the whole unit; LCD and capacitors rated to match."
  • "Carton and inner box certified for low-temperature brittleness (cold-crack test on file)."
  • "Sealed, desiccant-packed carton to stop condensation during the warm-up."

One receiving rule

On arrival, let units warm to room temperature 12–24 hours before powering on. Cold starts fake the "dead screen" across a whole batch.

Shipping to a cold market?

Tell us your route and winter lows — we'll spec a −25/−30°C storage rating, cold-crack-tested cartons and a warm-up SOP so your arrivals don't look 'dead'.
